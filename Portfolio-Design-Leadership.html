Exactly the right amount of understanding and control.

Case Study 01 · Customer Impact

Removing 30 days of friction

30 days → 1h 48m
Director of Customer Experience
DF Capital Bank  |  2020 – 2023
Problem

Onboarding a new commercial customer took the industry a month, and longer still for new customers with no track record. Every day of waiting was a day a customer's plans were on hold.

Strategy

Redesign the system, not the interface. An online application journey, automation of sales and decisioning, human-in-the-loop coordination, and fundamental process redesign, including immediate underwriting decisions.

Outcome

Same-day activation at scale, record of 1 hour 48 minutes. NPS 85 for onboarding. New customer growth +34%, breaking the 1,000-dealer milestone.

One customer, let down by another provider, had equipment arriving that day with no finance in place. We onboarded, activated and funded them the same day, meaning they retained their strong cash flow position by financing the equipment, and used their own cash to grow the business instead. Fast and human: the team visited them in person to walk them through it.

Case Study 02 · Quality at Scale

How do you maintain quality when AI makes production almost free?

Director of Customer & User Experience
SS&C Blue Prism  |  Sep 2023 – Present
Problem

One platform, 30 product domains, and experiences drifting apart as an AI-first operating model arrived faster than the culture could absorb. Production was becoming cheap. Consistency and craft were at risk, and customers were starting to feel the seams.

What I built

A design system that enforces quality: 31 component packages with accessibility and consistency standards built in, so every team ships the same standard of experience. Around it: structured crits, design QA on shipped product, and automated checks that catch problems before customers ever see them.

What changed

Quality became something people inherit rather than inspect. Design QA on shipped product surfaced a systemic consistency problem, which I took to the executive team with a proposed remediation. And on the receiving end: designers get quality by default instead of arguing for it, engineers ship the same standard of UI without interpretation, and customers get a product that feels like one product, not thirty. An AI-augmented delivery model, from prototype through spec to starter code, is now adopted across UX, Engineering and QA.

Case Study 03 · Product Vision

One governed home for every asset

Director of Customer & User Experience
SS&C Blue Prism  |  Defined problem, solution and visuals with Product
Problem

The platform grew fast. It started as an RPA vendor, then branched out rapidly into new asset types: agents, workflows, digital workers, forms, chat experiences, connectors. An efficient AI process meant product teams were shipping as fast as possible to close competitor gaps and move ahead in the market. With that pace came a challenge: there was no unified way to create, display, monitor and control the different asset types, and every new one risked becoming its own siloed experience.

Point of view

Never a different experience for every asset type. One set of unified patterns for creating, displaying, monitoring and controlling every asset, so familiarity and confidence carry across everything. And it had to happen at shipping pace, not as a separate programme: slowing the new features down to fix consistency would have cost the sales that depended on them.

Design

Asset Hub as the central unified repository for every asset type. Unified studio environments, so creating anything feels familiar whatever it is. A unified Control Center for monitoring the performance of every asset. The hard questions were horizontal: where do we surface, create, manage and control assets across the whole platform?

Outcome

Today the platform operates as a single governed estate: every asset lives in one governed place, with its lifecycle, permissions and release path managed centrally, and every asset type created, managed and monitored through the same patterns. The challenge any growing platform faces is the same: how do you add capability without losing familiarity, confidence and ease?

The Shift · Leading Through It

What I learned when speed started squeezing craft

I built the AI-augmented delivery model in the last case study. It unlocked pace—less handoff pain, less interpretation, and more output with less effort.

But it also taught me where the line is. When speed is chased without discipline, technical debt accumulates quietly. The product becomes the compromise, and the best people leave.

The answer isn't to slow down, but to increase the precision of the start: code-based prototypes to set the bar, minimum standards to ensure speed has a floor, and a fast-follower roadmap to close debt in weeks, not years.

The deeper lesson: rework is the slow path. Design-led quality up front is how you actually ship quickly and well at once.

Removal is only an act of design when you know precisely which parts are unnecessary. Knowing that is the discipline I spend most of my time protecting in my team.

That trade-off is why I want to lead somewhere craft and customer focus are the organising principle, where excellence isn't traded for speed, and where the next generation of money experiences is being built.
